s1|In part one, reinforcement learning nearly doubled a nine-billion-parameter model on competitive programming. Then it stopped. Three different recipes, one ceiling, at about twelve.
s2|Most of the gain was not better algorithms. It was answers that compile and run.
s3|Ambitious search code fails to compile more often, but when it earns points, it earns more. A mean reward sees the risk, not the upside. So training removed the search.
s4|The model often names the better algorithm, then ships the safe one. Across its checkpoints, the best of fifty-five samples averages twenty-nine. A single sample, about twelve.
s5|Rewarding the best answer didn't lift the average. In theory, with deterministic scores, collapsing onto one program is that objective's optimum.
s6|As an agent, even untrained, the model used the judge as its test suite. Training added submissions, not testing.
s7|So now the oracle is scarce: ten submissions, and only a score band as feedback. Will the search move inside the model? That run has just started.
s8|Reinforcement learning learns what you pay for. Next, we change what we pay for.
